# Competitive Landscape

*Researched 2026-07-14*

## The two nearby camps

**Movement-snack / desk apps** — closest in *format*: Wakeout (2,000+ desk exercises, ex-Apple App of the Year), Daily Movement Snacks, stand-up reminder apps. All about sedentary health and energy. None ask how you feel; none measure anything mental.

**Mental-health fitness apps** — closest in *purpose*: Brain-Body Therapy (movement/somatics for anxiety — nearest conceptually, but program-style), Motion (mood + movement tracking), Moodfit (CBT tools). None do the 60-second matched dose.

## Nobody does the combination

Mood-matched mechanism (count-load for rumination, adrenaline burn for wired…) + one minute + invisible desk mode + pre/post anxiety delta + self-calibrating dose + a real human voice. That's the whitespace.

## Why the whitespace might exist (honest version)

Either nobody executed it yet, **or** anxiety relief is episodic and retention is hard — people churn when they feel better. That's why the badges/streaks/"knows you" layer matters more here than in most apps. The waitlist test measures desire; retention is the question after it.

## Moat

Not the idea (public research, visible product). The moat is: Jonny's voice, the early audience/waitlist, calibration insights, brand warmth, and speed. Wakeout has existed for years and didn't build this.


## Headspace's actual early protocol (Wayback, getsomeheadspace.com, Oct 2012–Jan 2013 — researched 2026-07-24)

What their site said and sold at roughly our stage:

- **Positioning headline:** meditation defined for normal life ("meditation for modern living"), with three plain bullets: bite-sized tools · delivered daily · web & mobile. Their framing elsewhere: the world's first "gym membership for the mind," and a "365-day Headspace Journey."
- **The free layer had a NAME and a NUMBER: Take10** — 10 minutes a day for 10 days, free, no card, presented as a *programme you complete*, not a "trial." The hero CTA was GET STARTED NOW with a handwritten-style "for free!" annotation. The free thing is a finite structured course; finishing it is the natural upgrade moment.
- **The paid layer was an everyday membership:** "Get Headspace everyday" from **£3.74/month** (annual billing) — the gym-membership metaphor doing the pricing work. Benefits ringed the product shot as life outcomes (sleep better, fight stress, increase focus, energy, self-control), not features.
- **Protocol in one line: free named foundation course → everyday membership, framed as a gym for the mind.** No lifetime deals, no discounts on the homepage, one price anchor.

**Settle translation (the "Headspace for movement" playbook):**
1. Our current free site ≈ their Take10 — but ours is unnamed and endless. Roadmap idea: give the free layer a name + completion arc ("your first ten snacks") so finishing it creates the membership moment. → logged in [[06 Ideas & Backlog]] territory.
2. "movement snacks for your mind" is already the exact structural parallel of "meditation for modern living." Keep.
3. Their price anchor at our stage: ~£3.74–4.99/mo annual (≈ $5–6). Today they charge $12.99/mo / $69.99/yr with a 14-day trial. Founding-price target zone for Settle: $3–6/mo annual-billed.
4. What we should NOT copy: their phrase-level copy (it's theirs, and our voice is ours) and lifetime-free promises they never made — their free thing was finite and named, their paid thing was priced from day one on the homepage.


## ✅ VERIFIED 2026-07-28 — what Headspace actually gave away (this changed our model)

Checked against primary-ish sources, not memory. **Headspace never gave away an unlimited
library.**

**2013.** Take10 was the free content — ten sessions, registration required, repeatable
forever. Beyond that the free layer was *support material, not product*: mindfulness podcasts,
videos of other beginners describing their experience, a meditation timer, and the community
forum. The moment you wanted more actual practice you paid — Take15, Take20 and the longer
series were subscription-only.
([proforganizer day-by-day review, 2013](https://proforganizer.com/website-reviews/headspace_pt_1/))

**Today.** More severe. After the trial expires there is **no free in-app content at all**.
Everything free lives outside the product: YouTube, the podcast, articles, Netflix, social.
([Headspace Help Center](https://help.headspace.com/hc/en-us/articles/115008192928-What-s-included-for-free))

**The mapping this forced on us:**

| Headspace | Settle |
|---|---|
| Take10 — free, repeatable, registration to start | **Take·5** |
| Take15 / Take20 / the Journey — paid from day one | **the mood-matched snack library** |
| Podcasts, animations, YouTube — free marketing | our reels, the IG character series, the emails |

We had it backwards: the snack library was being treated as "Take10's repeatable days" when it
is structurally the thing they *charged* for.

## Calm — researched 2026-07-28. More relevant to us than Headspace, in places.

- **They validated with a dumb free web toy before building anything.** ~2011, pre-Calm, Alex
  Tew put up "Do Nothing for 2 Minutes" — a sunset, a two-minute timer that reset if you
  touched anything, and an email capture. Hundreds of thousands of visits, 100k+ Facebook
  likes, press. **That is exactly what our reels and the old snack site are** — acquisition,
  not product.
- **Their free tier is stingy and cleverly shaped:** unguided timed meditations, **Day 1 of
  every multi-day program** (permanently — this is where our ungated Day 1 comes from), one
  breathing exercise, exactly one Sleep Story ("Blue Gold"), and background scenes. *7 Days of
  Calm is not free upfront* — it unlocks after you finish the trial and decline, i.e. they use
  free content as a **win-back for churned trials**, not as an entry point.
  ([Calm Help Center](https://support.calm.com/hc/en-us/articles/360044707294-What-Free-Content-is-Available-on-the-Calm-App))
- **They quadrupled their price and nothing happened**: $10/yr → $40/yr, "almost no drop-off in
  new sign-ups." Directly contradicts the instinct to price low.
- **Sleep Stories came from a timestamp** — they spotted a global 11 PM usage spike, realised
  people were using meditations to fall asleep, and built their biggest product from it.
  *Lesson: read our checkin/pixel data for the UNEXPECTED pattern, not just the designed funnel.*
- **Daily Calm** — one fresh 10-minute session every day — is the retention engine and the
  cleanest justification for a subscription: you're paying for newness, daily.
- **Lifetime tier (~£400)** for cash and to segment the committed. Relevant to the founding ask.
- Time-based 7-day trial vs Headspace's usage-based 10-session gate — two different philosophies.
  ([growth breakdown](https://growthclassics.beehiiv.com/p/calm-2-billion-growth-story) ·
  [strategy comparison](https://goodbetterbest.substack.com/p/how-calm-won-wellness))

**Two things we can't copy:** both sold into a category with enormous existing search demand
("meditation" was typed by millions). *"Movement snacks for anxiety" has essentially none* — so
we are more dependent on paid creative and slower to build organic than either of them. And
Calm's celebrity flywheel is not a lever we have.

**What transfers today:** validate with a free toy · gate the product · price higher than feels
comfortable · read the data for surprises.
