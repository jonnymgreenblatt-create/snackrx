# Take10 → Take·5 — the deep audit (2026-07-26)

*What the real Take10 actually was (book + app, 2011–2015, primary-ish sources), checked beat-by-beat against our `take5-spec.md` and `take5-scripts.md` draft 1. Verdict up front: our skeleton already matches theirs closely — the spec's Wayback research held up. But the deep dive surfaced **one missing beat, one misplaced metaphor, and four smaller upgrades**, plus firm answers to two of the spec's open questions. Structure is stolen; not one phrase is.*

---

## 1. What Take10 actually was (now verified in detail)

### The session skeleton (identical every day)

From the printed exercise in *Get Some Headspace* (2011) and a 2013 day-by-day reviewer of the app:

| # | Take10 beat | What happened | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Getting ready** | Chair, back straight, feet flat, hands in lap. Five deep breaths, in nose / out mouth, eyes close on the last exhale. | ~1 min |
| 2 | **Checking in** | Feel weight/contact points, acknowledge sounds, head-to-toe scan (tension pass, then ease pass), notice mood without fixing it. | ~5 min "to begin with" (book); compressed in the app |
| 3 | **The practice** | Breath counting 1→10, begin again; wander → gently return. | ~3–4 min |
| 4 | **Let go** | ~20 seconds of NOTHING — no technique, mind roams free. Book: "Don't try to control it in any way." Kept deliberately short for beginners. | ~20–30s |
| 5 | **Return** | Reverse the opening: back to body, back to sounds, eyes open — then *notice how you feel vs. when you started* and carry it into the next activity. | ~1 min |

### The rules around the skeleton (all now source-confirmed)

- **The skeleton never varied; the day-intro did.** Each day's intro was one of four kinds: *instruction, reminder, encouragement, or common-mistakes pointer*. Concept animations (1–2 min: blue sky, traffic, wild horse) appeared "every few days," not daily. Day 1 ran ~30 min with welcome videos — heavily front-loaded orientation.
- **Silence grew.** Narration measurably thinned across the 10 days; by day 5–6 users reported being "in the groove"; paid levels expanded the silences further.
- **Real length overran the number**: sessions ran 12–15 min despite "10." The number was a promise of smallness, not a stopwatch.
- **One metaphor per concept, seeded early, called back late.** Blue sky (calm isn't created, it's revealed), traffic on the road (watch thoughts, don't chase the cars), wild horse (give it space, shorten the rope gently). These 1-minute animations did the teaching — and later became Headspace's marketing library.
- **Hard-won tone rules**: one voice, no music, no jargon, no mysticism. "No such thing as good or bad meditation, just aware or unaware." Humor and play encouraged. Never promise results ("give up the expectation for wonderful things").
- **Gating: one new session per calendar day**, sequential, old days free to repeat forever ("it's important to have a sleep between each session to fully absorb it" — their own guidance). Never called a trial.
- **Completion → paywall was their sore spot.** Period reviewers who loved the course still resented the day-10 wall ("pony up a bunch of money"). The upgrade moment worked; the resentment was real and documented.
- **The book's third pillar was integration**: "dot-to-dot" mindfulness attached to mundane daily acts (teeth, shower, walking). The course taught the technique; integration made it a habit.

## 3. Proposed revisions (structure and rhythm only — zero borrowed phrasing)

### 3.1 THE BIG ONE — add the "Still Moment" (their Let-Go beat, made movement-native)

Take10's most distinctive beat: ~20 seconds of deliberate *nothing* between practice and close. We skipped it. For us it's even more load-bearing than for them, because **the felt before/after contrast IS our product** — it's what the app's settled-scale measures. The course should ritualize feeling it.

**Proposal — new shared line S4, played after every day's practice, before the close:**

> S4 · The still moment: "Now be still for a moment. Let everything settle, and just notice what's humming."

Then silence. And here's where **"silence grows" becomes concrete instead of aspirational**: Day 1 ≈ 5 seconds of stillness · Day 2 ≈ 8s · Day 3 ≈ 12s · Day 4 ≈ 15s · Day 5 ≈ 20s. One recorded line, five silence durations — the training wheels come off on a schedule, exactly their mechanic, at our scale. Costs one clip.

### 3.2 Settle-in: add the grounding half-line (and recognize the check-in we already have)

Their opener grounded the body in contact points before anything else. S1 revision (one added beat):

> "Take five. Sit tall, feel your feet on the floor, let your hands rest, and take one slow breath."

And their 5-minute check-in? We shouldn't add one — at our length it would eat the day. But note it's not missing: **the app's pre-snack settled-scale rating IS a check-in** ("notice your mood without fixing it," compressed to one slider). When Take·5 gets its on-site card, the day should open with the same rating the snacks use — course and app share the instrument, and course days feed the same before/after data.

### 3.3 Move the "dials" metaphor from Day 5 to Day 1 — it's our blue sky

Headspace seeded blue sky early and spent ten days paying it off. Our equivalent spine metaphor — *your body has dials, and your mind reads them constantly* — is currently revealed as a Day 5 twist. Flip it: **Day 1 intro opens the course with the dials frame** ("This week we'll find five of them. Today's is hiding low in your calf."), each close names the day's dial, and Day 5 becomes the payoff it wants to be ("five dials, and you turned every one") instead of introducing the idea cold. D5's current text barely changes; D1 gains two sentences.

### 3.4 Rotate the intro *kinds*: one reassurance beat mid-course

All five draft intros are pure teach. Take10 rotated in encouragement and common-mistakes days — the reassurance was structural, not incidental. Cheapest fix: **Day 3's intro opens with a two-line common-mistakes beat** (day 3 = where drop-off lives, and it's already "the quietest practice"):

> "Day three. Quick word first. If you missed a day, or felt a little silly doing this — that's normal, and it costs you nothing. There's no doing this wrong. Only doing it."

("No doing it wrong" is our house analog of their "no good or bad meditation, just aware or unaware" — same job, our words. It also already lives in S3's DNA: "nothing to get right.")

### 3.5 Systematize integration: every close names ONE everyday trigger

The book's third pillar — attach the technique to a mundane daily moment. D2's close already does this instinctively ("any time you catch your shoulders climbing, you know the way down"). Make it the rule for all five:

- D1: "Next time you're stuck sitting — a call, a show, a waiting room — let the heels rise."
- D2: ✅ already has it (shoulders climbing).
- D3: "Red light. Loading screen. That's your cue: teeth apart."
- D4: "When the workday ends, that's a good minute to trade hands."
- D5: "Any doorway you walk through: out slow, eyes up."

(Ilustrative drafts — Jonny's register wins.) This is the habit engine the course leaves behind, and it costs one sentence per close.

### 3.6 De-risk the completion moment (their one documented failure)

Take10 graduates hitting a hard paywall produced the only consistent bitterness in otherwise glowing reviews. Our D5 close is already structurally safer ("you don't need me for any of this anymore") — one addition makes it airtight: **say the free site stays free, explicitly, at the moment of the founding ask.** The three-rung ladder promises it; the completion screen should too. Suggested shape: everything you've met this week stays free at settlemoves.com — the membership is for what comes next, and founding members go first.

### 3.7 Keep (already matching, don't touch)

S1 as unchanging ritual · teach-then-do intros ~70s · practices on existing snack assets · S2's notice-the-difference close · under-running the number (~4 real min; hard rule: no day ever exceeds 5) · one voice, no music · exclamation + settled-scale rules · "five little experiments, and you're the scientist" (that's our humor-and-play rule, native).

## 4. The spec's open questions — now answered by evidence

1. **Practice length (60s vs ~90s for days 3–4):** Take10's number overran reality and nobody cared until the paywall. Verdict: let days 3–4 run ~90s; protect the 5-minute ceiling, not the 60-second floor.
2. **Day-gating:** Headspace hard-gated one new session per day, old days repeatable, with an honest stated reason ("sleep between sessions to absorb"). It worked for a decade. Verdict: **gate all days daily — drop the bingeable-days-1–2 compromise.** The email drip IS the gate in v1 (a day arrives when its email arrives), so v1 gets this for free; keep the same rule when the on-site card ships. Old days always repeatable.
3. **Animations for jaw/hands days:** their concept videos were 1–2 min and appeared "every few days," not daily — text+orb pacing for v1 is period-accurate. Animations are a later polish (see §5 — build them as reels first).
4. **Pre-verdict use as the iteration lever:** unchanged by this research; decision already made (after test readout).

## 5. The strategic freebie: their animation library = our reels lane

Take10's concept animations (blue sky, traffic) did double duty for a decade: in-course teaching AND the marketing library (their YouTube/ads still run on them). We are already accidentally building the same asset class — the bloom reel *is* a concept animation for Day 2's shoulder drop, and the welcome email already carries it inline. The pattern to make deliberate: **each Take·5 day eventually gets one 20–30s stylized concept piece (second heart, the drop, unclench, hands, dials) that lives in the drip email, in the course, AND as an organic reel.** One asset, three jobs, house style. The dials piece practically storyboards itself.

---

## 6. What we deliberately did NOT adopt

- **Their content.** Breath-counting meditation is their product; movement is ours. (The bloom-reel lesson stands: watching is theirs, doing is ours.)
- **Their phrasing.** Everything above is structural; scripts stay in Jonny's register. Blue sky, traffic, wild horse stay theirs — we have the second heart and the dials.
- **The 5-minute check-in** — at our scale it would be the whole session; the settled slider carries it.
- **Overrunning the number** — they got away with 12–15 real minutes on a "10" promise; we won't test that patience. Under-promise stays.
- **Music-free is theirs AND ours already** — coincidence of taste, kept.
